## "Which annual number is your claim about?"

The main message uses BMKG's own release figures: total 8.264 (2020) → 43.439 (2025), and M5+ 244 → 153. That is the number reporters print, together with BMKG's own split of it.

The public catalog is the evidence for *why*. Its yearly totals compared with BMKG's year-end announcements:

| Year | BMKG announced | Catalog (in box) | Difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2020 | 8.264 | 8.283 | +0,2% |
| 2021 | 10.570 | 10.460 | −1,0% |
| 2022 | 10.792 | 10.390 | −3,7% |
| 2023 | 10.789 | 11.277 | +4,5% |
| 2024 | 7.358 (31 Des) / 29.869 (17 Jan 2025) | 7.498 | +1,9% vs year-end |
| 2025 | 43.439 | 18.592 | catalog = 43% |

Through 2024 the catalog *is* BMKG's year-end number. The 2025 total and the second 2024 figure have no public event-level breakdown, which is why decision 1 points reporters to the M5+ and felt counts BMKG already publishes.

## "Couldn't the magnitude-label switch explain claim 2?"

**Total counts are label-free.** Changing labels cannot create events, and the total still doubled.

**Within one magnitude type, the same pattern appears:**

| Type, periods | Events/yr | M4+/yr | Most common M | Median phase readings (M4,0–4,9) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| MLv, 2014–2017 → 2025 | 4.785 → 17.241 | 1.216 → 1.393 | 3,4 → 2,7 | 18 → 42 |
| M, 2010–2012 → 2020–2022 | 4.682 → 9.027 | 1.868 → 2.141 | 3,4 → 3,1 | 16 → 35 |

**In years where both types coexist, they agree.** Medians: 2012 3,5 vs 3,5; 2019 3,5 vs 3,4; 2023 3,3 vs 3,3; 2024 3,4 vs 3,2. A label switch cannot move the peak by 0,4.

**There is no downward drift against moment magnitude (Mw).** Catalog magnitude minus Mw: +0,04 (2014–2017, n = 147) and +0,16 (2022–2025, n = 1.265). If anything, later magnitudes read slightly *higher*, which would inflate later M4+ counts, not hide them.

**What we cannot rule out:** a change in how *small*-quake magnitudes are computed within the same type, because small quakes have no Mw to check against. That is why F3 and F4 say "consistent with better detection".

## "The UGM thesis says completeness was Mw 4,4."

- Diantari (2017) estimated Mc at Mw 4,4 for 2008–2016, using a declustered catalog converted to Mw, and found no effect of network growth in that period.
- If M4 was incomplete in our 2014–2017 baseline, then early M4+ counts are too *low*, so our +10% *overstates* any real increase.
- The network expansion we point to comes after 2016, outside their period.

## Robustness checks

- **Excluding 2025** (2021–2024 vs 2014–2017): 91% of the rise is below M4, and M4+ rose 20%, against a 76% rise in the total.
- **With 2009–2012 as the baseline:** the total rose 125%, and M4+ fell 7% (2.309 → 2.145).
- **M5+ in the catalog:** 327, 173 and 189 per year (2009–2012, 2014–2017, 2022–2025).
- **M6+ in the catalog:** 21, 5,5 and 16,5 per year. These are too few to trend, and one sequence moves them.
- **F4 has two valid comparisons:**
  - On the chart: period medians, 20 → 44.
  - Yearly: 2019 → 2022, 22 → 44, which is where the jump happens.
- **Felt and damaging quakes** (BMKG releases): felt 754 → 973 (+29%) and damaging 11 → 25 from 2020 to 2025. Damaging has been flat at 20–25 since 2021.
  - Both rose far less than the total (×5,3).
  - Felt counts also depend on where quakes strike and on reporting.
- **BMKG's M5+ and the catalog's M5+ differ** (e.g. 2021: 244 vs about 200). Releases may use revised magnitudes, so the two series are never mixed in one figure.
